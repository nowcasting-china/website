News is the released value minus the model's forecast of it, in the unit of the series (percentage points for growth rates, index points for surveys). The weight is the effect on the nowcast, in percentage points, of one unit of news. The impact is weight times news, computed for each posterior draw and then averaged, so it is close to, but not exactly, the product of the two columns. The annual rate is the mean of the four quarterly YoY rates: released quarters at the official number, open quarters at the model estimate. A release's impact on the annual rate is its impact on each decomposed quarter divided by four, and its weight is aggregated the same way; news, weight and impact are defined as on the nowcast tab and in the FAQ. When a quarter's GDP is published, the official number replaces the model's backcast: that row has the official number minus the backcast as news and a weight of 1/4. The remainder is the change in the estimate not explained by the listed releases: re-estimation of the model between updates, data revisions, and any open quarter not decomposed at that date. On the chart, the markers are the forecasts for that year published by the IMF, OECD, ADB and World Bank, at their publication dates; the dashed red line or band is the government target, from the day it was announced.

What data does the model use?

Eleven series: ten monthly or quarterly indicators of Chinese activity and the target, real GDP growth. All enter as year-on-year growth rates, except the surveys, which enter as index levels.

How the series are selected. We start from what market participants follow: the relevance scores Bloomberg assigns to Chinese releases and the impact levels of the ForexFactory calendar. Among the high-relevance indicators we keep those that are published quickly, cover a long history, and have real-time vintages, so that the model can be evaluated on the data as they were known at each date. The panel spans industry, consumption, trade, transport and business surveys, and combines hard data with surveys, which are less precise but come out first.

The Spring Festival. Since 2012 the NBS publishes industrial production, electricity, construction and retail sales as one combined value for January and February. The model keeps that value and links it to its January and February estimates through an aggregation equation (below).

What is the methodology?

A Bayesian dynamic factor model with a time-varying mean, heterogeneous dynamics, stochastic volatility and outliers. The equations for series \(i\) in month \(t\) are the following.

\[ y_{i,t} = \mu_{i,t} + \lambda_{i,0} f_t + \lambda_{i,1} f_{t-1} + \dots + \lambda_{i,m} f_{t-m} + e_{i,t} \] \[ \mu_{i,t} = \mu_{i,t-1} + \eta_{i,t}, \qquad \eta_{i,t} \sim N(0, \sigma^2_{\mu,i}) \] \[ f_t = \phi_1 f_{t-1} + \dots + \phi_p f_{t-p} + u_t, \qquad u_t \sim N(0, \sigma^2_{f,t}) \] \[ e_{i,t} = \varepsilon_{i,t} + o_{i,t}, \qquad \varepsilon_{i,t} = \rho_{i,1} \varepsilon_{i,t-1} + \dots + \rho_{i,q} \varepsilon_{i,t-q} + \sigma_{i,t} v_{i,t}, \qquad o_{i,t} = \gamma_{i,t} \zeta_{i,t}, \; \gamma_{i,t} \in \{0,1\} \] \[ \log \sigma^2_{i,t} = \phi_{h,i} \log \sigma^2_{i,t-1} + \omega_{i,t}, \qquad \log \sigma^2_{f,t} = \phi_{h,f} \log \sigma^2_{f,t-1} + \omega_{f,t} \]

Notation.

  • \(y_{i,t}\): series \(i = 1, \dots, n\) in month \(t\), as it enters the model (growth rate or survey level).
  • \(\mu_{i,t}\): the time-varying mean of series \(i\), with innovation variance \(\sigma^2_{\mu,i}\).
  • \(f_t\): the common factor; \(\lambda_{i,0}, \dots, \lambda_{i,m}\): the loadings of series \(i\) on the factor and its first \(m\) lags.
  • \(\phi_1, \dots, \phi_p\): the autoregressive coefficients of the factor, of order \(p\); \(u_t\): its shock, with time-varying variance \(\sigma^2_{f,t}\).
  • \(e_{i,t}\): the idiosyncratic component of series \(i\), the sum of an autoregressive part \(\varepsilon_{i,t}\) of order \(q\), with coefficients \(\rho_{i,1}, \dots, \rho_{i,q}\), and an outlier term \(o_{i,t}\).
  • \(\sigma_{i,t}\): the time-varying standard deviation of the idiosyncratic shock \(v_{i,t}\); \(\gamma_{i,t}\): the indicator of an outlier in month \(t\), of size \(\zeta_{i,t}\).
  • \(\phi_{h,i}, \phi_{h,f}\): the persistence of the log-volatilities; \(\omega_{i,t}, \omega_{f,t}\): their shocks.
  • Common factor \(f_t\): the signal shared by all series, with its own stochastic volatility \(\sigma^2_{f,t}\). Each series responds to it with its own lags, \(\lambda_{i,0}, \dots, \lambda_{i,m}\), so leading indicators and lagging ones can coexist.
  • Time-varying mean \(\mu_{i,t}\): a random walk that absorbs the secular slowdown of Chinese growth without forcing it into the factor.
  • Idiosyncratic component \(e_{i,t}\): series-specific dynamics with stochastic volatility \(\sigma^2_{i,t}\), plus an outlier term \(o_{i,t}\) that is switched on only in rare months, such as the pandemic, so that a few extreme observations do not drive the estimates.
  • Quarterly series (GDP, the business climate index) are linked to the monthly latent variables by summing the three months of the quarter. Combined January-February releases enter as \(\bar g_t = w\, g_t^{\mathrm{Jan}} + (1-w)\, g_t^{\mathrm{Feb}}\) with \(w = 1/2\).

Estimation. The model is estimated by Gibbs sampling. The priors are calibrated on a presample, from the start of the data to December 2006, which is not used otherwise. The nowcast is the posterior predictive distribution of GDP growth; the bands are its 68% and 80% intervals.

Impact of releases. Each update compares the new releases with what the model expected and attributes the change in the nowcast to each of them. The next section gives the algebra.

Evaluation. The model is evaluated in real time: at every release date since 2008 it is re-estimated on the data available on that day, and its predictions are scored against the GDP figure published later.

How is the impact of a data release computed?

Between two updates the nowcast changes because new data arrive. The change is split into the contribution of each release, following Bańbura and Modugno (2014).

News. Let \(\Omega_{v-1}\) be the data available at the previous update and \(\Omega_v\) the data available now. For each newly released value \(x_j\), the news is the part the model did not expect:

\[ \nu_j = x_j - E\left[ x_j \mid \Omega_{v-1} \right] \]

The forecast \(E[x_j \mid \Omega_{v-1}]\) uses the parameters of the current estimation and the data of the previous update. A release equal to the model's forecast carries no news and does not move the nowcast, whether the number itself is high or low.

Weights. The revision of the nowcast of GDP growth in quarter \(Q\), \(y_Q\), is a weighted sum of the news:

\[ E\left[ y_Q \mid \Omega_v \right] - E\left[ y_Q \mid \Omega_{v-1} \right] = \sum_j w_j \, \nu_j, \qquad w = \mathrm{Cov}(y_Q, \nu) \, \mathrm{Var}(\nu)^{-1} \]

The weight \(w_j\) is the effect on the nowcast, in percentage points, of one unit of news in release \(j\). It is larger when the series is closely tied to GDP through the common factor, when its own noise is small at that date (the stochastic volatility lowers the weight of a series while it is noisy), and when little other information about the same months is already available. Releases published on the same day are treated jointly, so each weight takes the other releases into account.

Impact. The impact of release \(j\) is \(w_j \nu_j\). The model is Bayesian, so the news and the weights are computed for each posterior draw \(g = 1, \dots, G\) of the parameters and volatilities, and the impact is the average over the draws:

\[ \mathrm{impact}_j = \frac{1}{G} \sum_{g=1}^{G} w_j^{(g)} \, \nu_j^{(g)} \]

The tables report the average news, the average weight and the impact. The impact is an average of products, so it is close to, but not exactly, the product of the two columns; for a release with a very small weight the two can differ in sign.

Outliers. When the model reads a release as an outlier, the outlier component is removed before the news is computed. Such a release carries little news, as it does in the nowcast itself.

Revisions and other. The impacts account for the change that comes from the new releases. The nowcast also changes for other reasons: the model is re-estimated at every update, past data are revised, and the published nowcast is the median of the predictive distribution while the decomposition applies to its mean. These are grouped in "revisions & other", computed as a remainder:

\[ \mathrm{nowcast}_v - \mathrm{nowcast}_{v-1} = \sum_j \mathrm{impact}_j + \text{revisions and other} \]

Annual rate. The annual rate is the mean of the four quarterly rates, so the impact of a release on it is the sum of its impacts on the open quarters of the year, divided by four. Its weight is aggregated in the same way. When the GDP of a quarter is published, the official number replaces the model's backcast of that quarter: this adds (official number minus backcast) / 4, shown as a row of its own with a weight of 1/4.

Units and an example. The news is in the unit of the series: percentage points for growth rates, index points for surveys. Suppose industrial production growth comes out 0.5 percentage point below the model's forecast and its weight is 0.09. The impact is \(0.09 \times (-0.5) = -0.045\): the nowcast falls by about 0.05 percentage point.

Reference: M. Bańbura and M. Modugno (2014), "Maximum likelihood estimation of factor models on datasets with arbitrary pattern of missing data", Journal of Applied Econometrics 29(1), 133–160.

How often is the nowcast updated?

Every time a release in the panel comes out. On each release day the model is re-estimated with all the data published up to that day, and the nowcasts for the current quarter, the next quarter and the year are updated.
